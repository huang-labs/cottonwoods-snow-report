# Design

[← README](../README.md)

Shared layout, typography, data presentation and responsive behavior. Code paths in this guide are relative to the repository root.

Mobile-first and light by default, with a full dark theme chosen by the masthead’s sun/moon buttons (the system preference applies when no choice is saved).

- **Loading.** `src/components/loading/ReportLoading.tsx` owns initial report skeletons: shared colors, accessibility and motion, with layouts for snowfall, resorts, weather, plumes, roads and Daily Snow. Stable page titles appear immediately; decorative placeholders pulse after 500 ms and remain static with reduced motion. Ready data replaces them immediately. Initial request failures show automatic-retry copy; background polling keeps the last report visible. Direct Daily Snow archive links reuse the article skeleton, while edition switches retain the article already on screen.
- **One type scale.** `src/app/globals.css` defines size tokens (display, h2, title, h3, prose, body, meta, caption, label) and every page uses them: page titles are the display size, section headings h2, item names (resort names, the Daily Snow teaser title) the title size, timestamps and notes caption. Add a token rather than a new pixel size.
- **One page pattern.** Each page is a title with a muted subtitle, its sections, and its source line(s) at the foot (freshness chip, source time, last check, attribution). Freshness chips live in source lines and resort-page headers, not mid-sentence. The Resorts page (`/`) is the exception, kept deliberately quiet: no freshness chips, only each resort's report time in its timeline, then the value legend and the NWS forecast's source line. Unavailable or delayed required feeds still raise the global banner.
- **No card chrome.** Long-form pages (resort reports, Daily Snow) are page content: one reading width, no borders, sections separated by space. The pale panel fill is reserved for summaries: the Daily Snow teaser (reaching into the page gutters, edge-to-edge on phones), the next-storm box, the road-status box, resort snow tiles and code-drawn charts. Photos and plume images stand on their own.
- **Data marks.** Measured values are snow-blue, zeros gray; inch and degree marks are raised to the cap line. Status chips share semantic tones (green current, amber old/delayed, red unavailable/confirmed failure, neutral unknown); AI provenance stays blue. The AI resort pick is an accent line in the gutter spanning the picked resort’s timeline and its reasoning beneath.
- **Snowfall timeline.** `src/components/timeline/` draws one resort's snow days: the 7 days before the current one (each resort's morning 24 h report), its latest 24 h figure in the middle, and 7 days of NWS forecast for its canyon's grid point. A day is labeled by the morning it ends (12:00 UTC: 5 AM MST / 6 AM MDT), matching resort morning reports and NWS's 6-hour blocks. Reported bars are solid snow-blue; forecast bars are striped; a dashed outline behind a past bar is what NWS forecast for that day when it began. Bars show whole inches (“<1” for a trace, so it never reads as a reported zero) on a scale shared by every timeline on the page (6″–18″, taller bars clipped with a notch). Missing days keep their slot: “–” for an empty field, an unpublished total or no new morning report, and a light hatch for a day not recorded. The strip is static: no hover, selection or detail line. Each day carries a full description (date, amount, source window and report time, forecast outline) as screen-reader text. The component takes the page's heading and the line beneath it, so other pages can reuse it. There are no group totals. On the Resorts page each header is the resort's name (with the AI pick badge when endorsed) over “Utah · United States”; the middle column shows the 24 h figure and its report time, under one heading for every resort rather than each resort's own wording.
- **Snowfall windows.** Every resort page shows the same windows in the same order, under our labels (Overnight, 12 h, 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, 7 days, Storm, Season; `SNOW_LABEL` in `src/components/sections/resort-display.ts`). A window the resort doesn’t publish keeps its slot as “—”. The resort’s own wording appears only where its window differs (Alta’s 12 h for overnight, Solitude’s “since lifts closed”). The Resorts page shows only the 24 h figure; the other windows are on each resort's page. Source notes that cover several windows are listed once. Values are never derived: each number is the resort’s own.
- **Notices.** Resort alerts and road notices share one compact list (`NoticeList` in `ui.tsx`): title and text as one sentence, source and time beneath, most severe first. On resort pages they sit under the jump links, so the header block is identical on every resort.
- **Phones.** One breakpoint (900px). Snowfall timelines keep all 15 columns on a 360px phone (no sideways scrolling); the middle column becomes a narrow highlighted “24h / now” bar, and the report time moves to the right of the resort's name. Charts redraw at the screen width, so the NWS forecast table and six-hour snowfall charts scroll inside their own boxes.
